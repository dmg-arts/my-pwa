9ThirtyOne

Cadet and instructor feedback, in a Drive folder your detachment owns. dmg-arts.github.io/9thirtyone
Why

A detachment serves cadets from several Crosstown universities, so it cannot run on any one university's IT system. It stands up a Det Google Account for mail — and that account arrives with no course platform, no survey tool, nowhere for evaluations to live.

Leadership Laboratory has no feedback capture at all, and it is where cadets lead cadets. Upperclassmen improvise in consumer form tools: no oversight, no archive, nothing that outlasts the cadet who built it. That is a records gap, not a tooling gap.

How

A web page, not an installation. Cadets open a link, sign in with the Google account the detachment already mails them at, and answer — once each, on a phone, offline if need be.

The database is a Drive folder in the detachment's own Google account: ordinary files, readable without this app. If 9ThirtyOne stopped tomorrow, every response would still be sitting there. The app asks only for drive.file — files it created itself, and nothing else in that Drive.

Instructors, cadre and commanders

  • Build a form — a reusable set of questions — and issue it to a class, an event, or named cadets, with an open and a due date.
  • Read the analysis, not a stack. Distributions rather than averages, and questions where the room genuinely split are called out instead of averaged into the middle.
  • Chase without unmasking. Receipts are stored apart from answers, so the app can say who still owes feedback on an anonymous form.
  • Cadre get a second panel — the same screen pointed at folders an instructor cannot reach at all. Commanders also reach their own area there.
  • By‑instructor shows an instructor their own results, cadre the instructors they oversee, and a commander everyone.

What a cadet sees

  • A link, or a QR code on a projector. Tap it, sign in, done — nothing to configure and nothing to paste.
  • Only what is assigned to them. The cadet screen lists their open feedback requests and what is already submitted.
  • Words, not numbers. Ratings are chosen as words; the maths runs on the number underneath.
  • Once each. A second attempt at the same request is refused at the form.
  • No Drive access at all. A cadet is never asked for permission to their own Google Drive, and never gets access to the detachment's folder.
Written answers are screened

Written answers are checked for wording that may need a person's attention. It matches words, never meaning — so a clear screen is never proof that nothing was reported, and this is not a reporting channel.

Where an answer actually goes

Anonymous, and what that cannot do

Anonymous results stay hidden until three responses exist — below that, only the count of who took part is shown. But removing names cannot change what someone wrote: an answer describing a role only one person holds identifies its author anyway.
